The short version. RichNotify reads the notifications that arrive on your phone so it can show them properly on your watch. That content never leaves your two devices. There is no server, no account, no advertising, no analytics and no crash reporting, and the app has no internet permission at all.

Why that claim is checkable

Privacy claims are cheap, so here is how to check this one rather than believe it.

RichNotify's manifest does not request INTERNET or ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE. An Android app without INTERNET cannot open a network connection — not "does not", but cannot: the operating system refuses. You can confirm this in the permission list on the Play listing, or by inspecting the APK yourself.

This matters more here than in most apps. RichNotify can see every notification your phone receives: messages, one-time codes, bank alerts, health reminders. An app holding that and also holding network access would be asking for a great deal of trust. This one is built so that the trust is not needed.

The check is also automated. Every push to the project's repository runs a build that reads the merged manifest of both apps and fails if INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE or QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES has appeared — including by way of a dependency that declares one quietly.

What RichNotify reads

To place a notification on your watch, the app reads what the notification contains:

  • the app it came from, and that app's name and version
  • the title, the text, and any additional lines
  • the sender and the conversation, where the app provides them
  • attached images, stickers and voice messages
  • the notification's category, channel, and whether it can be replied to

It reads this through Android's notification access, which you grant in system settings and can withdraw at any time. The moment you withdraw it, RichNotify stops reading.

What it does with it. That content builds the card you see on your watch, groups messages into conversations, and applies the rules you have set. Nothing else.

What it does not read. RichNotify does not read your contacts, your accounts, your files, your location, your call log or your messages from within other apps. It holds no permission for any of them. It does not enumerate the apps on your device: it declares a narrow query for apps with a launcher icon, so it can offer you a list to pick from, and it does not hold QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES.

Where it goes

Your watch. The notification is sent over Android's Wear Data Layer to a watch you have paired with this phone. This is a direct link between your own two devices; it does not travel over the internet.

This phone's storage, only if you turn history on. History is off by default. With it off, no message is written down at all.

That default is deliberate. For an app that can read every message you receive, off is the honest starting point, and the app should have to be asked before it keeps a record.

Nowhere else. No server, no cloud backup, no analytics service, no crash reporter, no advertising network. There is no third party in this product to send anything to.

When history is on, stored notification content is encrypted with a key held in the Android Keystore, so it is not readable by simply reading the app's database file.

There is also a mode that keeps the shape without the words: it records that an app wrote and when, but not the title, the text or the sender.

You can name apps that are never recorded. Their notifications still reach your watch; nothing about them is written down, and excluding an app also erases what it already wrote. An individual conversation can be marked the same way.

What you can delete, and how

  • Delete all history now — erases every stored notification. Your rules and the record of which rule decided what remain.
  • Delete the decision records — erases the log of which rule decided what. After this, the diagnostics screen can no longer explain anything that already happened.
  • Forget a conversation — erases what is stored about it, including any name you gave it.
  • Clear the list on the watch — removes the cards held there.
  • Uninstall — removes everything the app stored, on both devices.

Deleting something here does not touch the app it came from, and deleting a message in that app does not bring it back here. RichNotify keeps the notification it saw, not the conversation itself.

Children

RichNotify is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone, of any age.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and this page is updated at the same time as the release it describes. A change that altered what leaves your device would be described plainly rather than folded into a version note.

Your rights

Because nothing is collected or transmitted, there is no account to close, no data to request from us, and nothing held anywhere for us to delete. Everything the app holds is on your own devices and is deletable by you, from inside the app, at any time.

What RichNotify cannot do

Written here rather than only in the store listing, because a limit that appears only where it is convenient is not honestly disclosed.

RichNotify can only show what a notification itself contains. If an app sends a terse notification and keeps the rest to itself, there is nothing to enrich. Replies, reactions and media appear only when the source app actually offers them, which varies by app and by version. Custom vibration patterns depend on your watch's manufacturer and are not guaranteed. And it cannot fix notifications that Android or your watch delivers late — no app can.
